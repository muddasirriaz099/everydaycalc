Lunch Break Calculator

By Muddasir Riaz · Updated November 12, 2026

Enter your shift times and when lunch starts to see when lunch ends, the time you work before and after it, and your paid hours with a paid or unpaid lunch.

If the shift ends earlier than it starts, we treat it as an overnight shift. Leave lunch length at 30 for a standard half-hour lunch.

Quick answer: To find when lunch ends, add the lunch length to the lunch start time: a 30-minute lunch that starts at 12:30 PM ends at 1:00 PM. To find paid hours, subtract an unpaid lunch from the shift: 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM with a 30-minute unpaid lunch is 8 paid hours. A paid lunch stays in your hours.

How to use the lunch break calculator

  1. Enter your shift start and end. Use the times you clock in and out. Overnight shifts work too.
  2. Enter when lunch starts and how long it lasts. The default is a 30-minute lunch.
  3. Choose paid or unpaid. Unpaid lunch is taken off your hours. Paid lunch is not.
  4. Press Calculate lunch break. You see the lunch end time, the time worked before and after lunch, and your paid hours.

How do you calculate a lunch break?

Two formulas cover almost every case:

Lunch end time = lunch start time + lunch length

Paid hours = (shift end − shift start) − unpaid lunch

For example, a shift from 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM is 8.5 hours on the clock. A 30-minute unpaid lunch leaves 8 paid hours. A 60-minute unpaid lunch leaves 7.5 paid hours. If lunch is paid, you are paid for all 8.5 hours.

Is lunch paid or unpaid?

It depends on whether you are free of work. Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, employers are not required to give meal or rest breaks. When they do, the rules from the U.S. Department of Labor are:

  • Short breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes are paid. They count as hours worked.
  • Meal periods of about 30 minutes or more can be unpaid if you are completely relieved of duty. If you must answer calls, watch a counter or stay at your desk, the time is generally paid.
  • Time you work through lunch must be paid. If your lunch is interrupted, the time may count as hours worked.

Many states have stricter rules than federal law. Check your state labor department's website for the exact requirements.

What are California's lunch break rules?

California requires meal periods for most non-exempt employees. According to the California Department of Industrial Relations:

  • A meal period of at least 30 minutes must be provided no later than the end of the employee's fifth hour of work. For example, if you start at 9:00 AM, lunch must begin by 2:00 PM.
  • A second meal period is required when an employee works more than 10 hours.
  • The first meal period can be waived by mutual consent only if the work period is no more than 6 hours. The second can be waived only if the total is no more than 12 hours and the first was not waived.
  • To be off duty, you must be relieved of all duty for the full 30 minutes. An on-duty meal period is allowed only in limited cases with a written agreement, and it is paid.
  • If a required meal period is not provided, the employer owes one extra hour of pay at the regular rate for each affected workday.

When should you take your lunch break?

There is no federal rule for the time of day. Most workers take lunch near the middle of the shift, usually after 4 to 5 hours of work. In an 8-hour shift starting at 8:00 AM, that is between 12:00 PM and 1:00 PM. Taking lunch too late can break state rules, such as California's fifth-hour deadline, and long stretches without a break can affect safety and focus. If you are planning a schedule, put lunch about halfway through the shift and check that it does not start after the legal limit in your state.

What about automatic lunch deductions?

Some time clocks subtract 30 minutes every day without asking whether you took lunch. This is allowed in many workplaces if employees really get an uninterrupted lunch, but it can cause underpayment when you work through it. Keep your own record of the times you clock in, clock out and take lunch. If your pay stub is shorter than your records, ask payroll to explain the difference. Common lunch break calculation mistakes

  • Subtracting lunch twice. If your clock times already exclude lunch because you clocked out for it, do not subtract it again.
  • Treating paid lunch as unpaid. A paid lunch does not reduce your hours.
  • Writing 7 hours 30 minutes as 7.30. The decimal is 7.5, because 30 minutes is half an hour.
  • Ignoring a second lunch on long shifts. In states such as California, shifts over 10 hours generally need another meal period.
  • Forgetting that working lunches are paid. If you work during lunch, the time counts.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate a lunch break?

Add the lunch length to the lunch start time to get the end time, and subtract an unpaid lunch from the shift to get paid hours. For example, 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM with a 30-minute unpaid lunch is 8 paid hours.

How many hours do you work with a 30-minute lunch?

Subtract 0.5 hours from the shift length. A shift from 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM is 8.5 hours on the clock and 8 paid hours with a 30-minute unpaid lunch.

Is a 30-minute lunch paid or unpaid?

Under federal law, a meal period of about 30 minutes or more can be unpaid if you are completely relieved of duty. If you have to work or stay on duty, the time is generally paid. Many states and employers have their own rules.

Are employers required to give lunch breaks?

The federal Fair Labor Standards Act does not require meal or rest breaks, but many states do. California, for example, requires a 30-minute meal period no later than the end of the fifth hour of work for most non-exempt employees.

When must lunch start in California?

No later than the end of your fifth hour of work, according to the California Department of Industrial Relations. If you start at 8:00 AM, lunch must begin by 1:00 PM. A second meal period is required when you work more than 10 hours.

What happens if my employer does not give me a required meal period in California?

The employer owes one additional hour of pay at your regular rate for each workday a required meal period was not provided. This is a premium payment and is not counted as hours worked for overtime.

Do I get paid for short breaks?

Yes. Short rest breaks of about 5 to 20 minutes are paid and count as hours worked under federal law.

Does this calculator work for night shifts?

Yes. If your shift end time is earlier than the start time, it is treated as an overnight shift that ends the next day. A lunch that starts after midnight is handled correctly.
